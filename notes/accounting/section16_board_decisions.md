# Section 16: Board Decisions — Practice Questions

**Avg exam weight: 6-8 pts | 3 past papers (not in Mar 2024, Jun 2024)**

> **Format**: Given projected financial statements comparing two scenarios (make vs buy, debt vs equity, with vs without CapEx). Students analyze financial ratios, cash effects, and strategic implications.
>
> **Magic triangle of financial ratios:**
> 1. **Cash/Liquidity** (cash ratio, quick ratio)
> 2. **Security** (equity ratio, debt ratio)
> 3. **Profitability** (gross profit margin, ROE, ROA)

## Q2: March 2025 (6 pts) — Hurlimann Ltd. (Debt vs Equity financing)

**Scenario**: Must replace old machine. New machine CHF 1 300 000. Both options pay CHF 300 000 cash, then either:
- **Debt financing**: Loan for remaining CHF 1 000 000
- **Equity financing**: Capital increase of CHF 1 000 000

Given: Planned balance sheets and P&L for 20_1 through 20_4.

**Questions:**

1. No vehicle purchases/sales. What is planned depreciation for vehicles in year 3?
2. What is the interest rate planned for the loan?
3. What is the saving in personnel expenses one year after purchase?
4. Why does "Cash" increase more than profit each year in equity financing?
5. Compare gross profit margin 20_1 vs 20_4.
6. Why is profit brought forward higher for equity financing in 20_4?

<details>
<summary>Show Solutions</summary>

1. **15** *(read from projected statements)*
2. **4.00%** *(calculate from interest expense and loan amount)*
3. **5** *(compare personnel expenses before/after machine purchase)*
4. **Depreciation affects profit but not cash** *(non-cash expense reduces profit but doesn't reduce cash)*
5. **Gross profit margin remains stable** *(revenue and COGS proportionally unchanged)*
6. **Additional debt increases interest expenses** *(equity financing has no interest burden, so higher cumulative profit)*

</details>

## Q3: January 2025 (6 pts) — Siriani Ltd. (With vs Without CapEx)

**Scenario**: Production company considering CHF 1 400 000 machine purchase. Financing: CHF 400 000 own cash + CHF 1 000 000 loan. Machine purchased at beginning of 20_2.

Given: Planned balance sheets and P&L for 20_1 through 20_4, comparing "without CapEx" vs "with CapEx".

**Questions:**

1. Annual depreciation for the new machine?
2. Annual interest for the loan?
3. In year 20_3, compare "CapEx" vs "no CapEx" ratios: Return on Sales, Equity ratio, Gross profit margin?
4. Which statement applies about the CapEx option?

<details>
<summary>Show Solutions</summary>

1. **10** *(calculated from projected depreciation schedule)*
2. **8** *(calculated from interest expense and loan)*

3. Ratio comparison (CapEx vs No CapEx in 20_3):
   - Return on Sale: **Higher** *(increased revenue eventually outpaces increased costs)*
   - Equity ratio: **Lower** *(additional loan increases liabilities)*
   - Gross profit margin: **Stable** *(revenue and direct costs change proportionally)*

4. **In the CapEx option, profit becomes temporarily lower as initially expenses increase relatively higher than revenues**

</details>

## Patterns & Traps

- **Always 3 exams out of 5** have this section (Jan 2025, Mar 2025, Jan 2026)
- **Depreciation doesn't affect cash**: This is the most tested concept — depreciation reduces profit but not cash
- **Debt vs equity financing**: Debt has interest expense (reduces profit), equity dilutes ownership but no interest
- **Cash is often the biggest risk**: New investments drain cash reserves
- **Gross profit margin often stays stable**: Because the investment affects operating costs below gross profit, not COGS
- **Read the projected statements carefully**: Most answers come directly from comparing numbers in the given tables
- **Magic triangle**: Always think about all three dimensions — a scenario can be better on one dimension but worse on another
- **CapEx calculations**: CapEx = difference in non-current assets between scenarios (if no other asset changes)
- **Interest rate**: Interest expense / Loan amount
