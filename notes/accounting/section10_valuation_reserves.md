# Section 10: Valuation Reserves (Hidden Reserves) — Practice Questions

**Avg exam weight: 4-5 pts | 4 past papers extracted (not in Jan 2026)**

> **Core concept:**
> - Valuation reserves = Internal value - External value (for assets) or External value - Internal value (for liabilities)
> - **Creating VR**: Understating assets or overstating liabilities → VR increases
> - **Dissolving VR**: Reversing the above → VR decreases
> - **External profit** = Internal profit + dissolved VR - newly created VR
> - Dissolving VR has **zero effect on cash** (purely accounting)

---

## Q1: March 2025 (4 pts) — Table-based MCQ

Given information (values in CHF '000):

| | Jan 1 external | Jan 1 internal | Jan 1 VR | Dec 31 external | Dec 31 internal | Dec 31 VR | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inventories | 56 | 84 | 28 | 64 | 96 | 32 | +4 |
| Furniture | 144 | 209 | 65 | 179 | 179 | 0 | -65 |
| Vehicles | 180 | 200 | 20 | 270 | 300 | 30 | +10 |
| Provisions | 22 | 20 | 2 | 11 | 10 | 1 | -1 |

**Q1a:** The increase of inventories in the balance sheet as per Dec 2024 is under-valued by 1/3. Effect on VR?

**Q1b:** Depreciation of Furniture is 30. All existing VR are fully dissolved as per Dec 31. No purchases or sales during the year. What is the balance of Furniture in the external balance sheet as per Dec 31?

**Q1c:** External Vehicles increases by 90. The VR percentage shall remain the same at year end. Effect on VR?

**Q1d:** Provisions are over-valued by 10%, both at beginning and end of year. Effect on VR?

<details>
<summary>Show Solutions</summary>

**Q1a: Increase of valuation reserves**
Under-valuing inventory increase means external value is lower than actual → creates more hidden reserves.

**Q1b: 179**
Internal value = 209 - 30 depreciation = 179. All VR dissolved means external = internal = 179.

**Q1c: Valuation reserves change by +10**
Beginning: VR = 20, external = 180, ratio = 20/200 = 10%. External goes to 270. To keep 10%: internal = 270 / 0.9 = 300. New VR = 300 - 270 = 30. Change = 30 - 20 = +10.

**Q1d: Decrease of valuation reserves**
Beginning: external provisions = 22, internal = 20, VR = 2. End: external = 11, internal = 10, VR = 1. Change = 1 - 2 = -1 → decrease.

</details>

## Q2: January 2025 (5 pts) — Three parts

### Part A (3 pts): A machine loses 10% of value each year (straight-line, 10 years useful life).

**Q2a:** Externally, declining balance depreciation at 20% of book value. How do VR change in year 2?

**Q2b:** Same declining balance at 20%. How do VR change in year 8?

**Q2c:** Externally, straight-line depreciation at 10% of purchase value. How do VR change in year 4?

### Part B (1 pt): Car purchase value CHF 80 000, VA of CHF 54 000. Sold for CHF 28 000. Booking entry to dissolve VR?

### Part C (1 pt): Internal profit = 24 000, dissolved VR on trading goods = 12 000, remaining VR on land = 80 000, newly added VR on furniture = 10 000. External profit?

<details>
<summary>Show Solutions</summary>

**Q2a: Increase**
Year 2: External depreciation (20% of 80%) = 16% of purchase price > Internal (10% of purchase price). Gap grows → VR increase.

**Q2b: Decrease**
Year 8: External depreciation (20% of a small book value) is now much less than internal (fixed 10%). Gap narrows → VR decrease.

**Q2c: No change**
Both internal and external use same 10% straight-line → identical depreciation, no VR created.

**Part B: Vehicle / Extraordinary income — 2 000**
External book value = 80 000 - 54 000 = 26 000. Sold for 28 000. Hidden reserves dissolved = 28 000 - 26 000 = 2 000.

**Part C: CHF 26 000**
External profit = Internal profit + dissolved VR - newly created VR = 24 000 + 12 000 - 10 000 = 26 000.

</details>

## Q3: March 2024 (5 pts) — Mark +/0/- effect on valuation reserves

| Business transaction | Effect? |
|---|---|
| A vehicle no longer used is sold above book value | ? |
| A new laptop booked as: IT expenses / Misc. current liabilities CHF 2 000 | ? |
| Invoice paid 20 days before payment term, by bank transfer | ? |
| A fully depreciated machine is still used for one more year | ? |
| An accrual lowering material expenses is set too low | ? |

<details>
<summary>Show Solutions</summary>

| Business transaction | Effect |
|---|---|
| Vehicle sold above book value | **-** *(hidden reserves in vehicle are dissolved on sale)* |
| Laptop expensed instead of capitalized | **+** *(asset not recognized externally → understated → VR created)* |
| Invoice paid early | **0** *(no effect on asset valuation)* |
| Fully depreciated machine still used | **-** *(internal value declines toward 0, gap narrows)* |
| Accrual for material expenses set too low | **+** *(expense not fully reduced → external profit lower → assets understated)* |

</details>

## Q4: June 2024 (4 pts) — Booking entries + calculations

**1.** Valuation reserves of CHF 30 000 are added to "Receivables from goods and services."

**2.** Valuation reserves of CHF 55 000 are dissolved on "Inventory, trading goods."

**3.** A machine bought 3 years ago for CHF 2 200 000. Externally: declining balance at 30% of book value. Internally: straight-line over 10 years. What are the VR after 2 years?

**4.** The CFO suggests dissolving the VR on the machine. By how much would cash change?

<details>
<summary>Show Solutions</summary>

**1. Losses from receivables / VA receivables from G&S — 30 000**
*(Creating hidden reserves on receivables: increase value adjustment)*

**2. Inventory, trading goods / Expenses for trading goods — 55 000**
*(Dissolving hidden reserves: increase inventory, reduce expense)*

**3. CHF 682 000**
- External (declining 30%): Year 1: 2 200 000 × 30% = 660 000 → BV = 1 540 000. Year 2: 1 540 000 × 30% = 462 000 → BV = 1 078 000.
- Internal (straight-line): 2 200 000 / 10 = 220 000/year. After 2 years: BV = 2 200 000 - 440 000 = 1 760 000.
- VR = 1 760 000 - 1 078 000 = **682 000**

**4. CHF 0**
Dissolving valuation reserves is a purely accounting entry — zero effect on cash.

</details>

## Patterns & Traps

- **External profit formula**: External profit = Internal profit + dissolved VR - newly created VR (dissolving VR *increases* external profit)
- **Declining balance vs. straight-line**: Early years → declining depreciates more → VR increase. Late years → declining depreciates less → VR decrease
- **Expensing an asset** (instead of capitalizing) → creates VR (asset understated externally)
- **Selling above book value** → dissolves VR (hidden reserves realized)
- **Provisions over-valued** = hidden reserves in provisions (liability side)
- **Cash effect of dissolving VR**: Always **zero** — purely accounting
- **Creating VR on receivables**: `Losses from receivables / VA receivables`
- **Dissolving VR on inventory**: `Inventory / Expense account`
