### 2.4 Trade discount when paying supplier

#### Questions

**Q1** [Jan 2026] — Henry's Treasure (Sole Proprietorship, Trading)
> Heinrich Huwyler pays the invoice of the Color Ltd. by bank transfer, after deducting 2% trade discount. The original invoice was CHF 3 400 minus CHF 200 return = CHF 3 200 outstanding. What are the booking entries?

**Q2** [Jan 2026] — Flint Ltd. (Production Company)
> The next day, after deducting 2% trade discount, Flint Ltd. pays the invoice of Corti Ltd. (CHF 6 000) by bank transfer. What are the booking entries?

**Q3** [Jan 2025] — GreenStopLynn (Sole Proprietorship, Trading)
> Lynn Becker pays the due invoice from the frozen good supplier, CHF 1 250, by bank transfer, after deducting 8% discount and 2% trade discount. What are the booking entries?

#### Answers

**A1** Two entries:
(1) Payables for Goods and Services / Expenses for Trading Goods CHF 64 (discount: 2% × 3 200)
(2) Payables for Goods and Services / Bank CHF 3 136 (payment: 3 200 − 64)
💡 Trade discount on payment requires TWO entries: first book the discount (reduces payables and expense), then book the net payment.

**A2** Two entries:
(1) Payables for Goods and Services / Expenses for Material CHF 120 (discount: 2% × 6 000)
(2) Payables for Goods and Services / Bank Account CHF 5 880 (payment: 6 000 − 120)
💡 Same principle for production company — discount reduces material expense, not trading goods expense.

**A3** Three entries:
(a) Discount: Payables for Goods and Services / Expenses for Trading Goods CHF 100 (8% × 1 250)
(b) Trade discount: Payables for Goods and Services / Expenses for Trading Goods CHF 23 (2% × (1 250 − 100) = 2% × 1 150)
(c) Payment: Payables for Goods and Services / Bank CHF 1 127 (1 250 − 100 − 23)
💡 Discount applied first, then trade discount on remaining amount. Three separate entries.

### 2.8 Equity calculations

#### Questions

**Q1** [Jan 2026] — Guido Grimm (Sole Proprietorship)
> The following facts are known: Equity at beginning of year CHF 100 000, Increase in equity during year CHF 10 000, Left-side surplus of account "Private" at year end CHF 5 000, Expenses for Trading Goods CHF 120 000, Expenses for wages/salaries for Guido Grimm CHF 50 000, Expenses for wages/salaries for Peter Stadler CHF 8 000, Financial expenses (bank loan interest) CHF 4 000, Financial expenses (own interest) CHF 5 000, Various expenses CHF 32 000, Profit of year CHF 17 200. What is the equity at the end of the year after closing the accounts?

**Q2** [Jan 2026] — Guido Grimm (Sole Proprietorship)
> Using the same data as Q1: What is the owner's income?

**Q3** [Jan 2026] — Guido Grimm (Sole Proprietorship)
> Using the same data as Q1: By which amount (incl. + or −) does the owner's income change if for both the financial expenses for the bank loan and the financial expenses for own interest the interest rate would have been 6% (instead of 5% which Guido Grimm was able to negotiate)?

**Q4** [Jan 2025] — Sven Eisenhut (Sole Proprietorship)
> Given: Salary for owner CHF 110 000, Interest for owner CHF 8 000, Cash withdrawals CHF 60 000, Equity increase during year CHF 20 000, Loss of period CHF 32 000, Equity in closing statement CHF 210 000. Calculate: (a) Equity at beginning of the year, (b) Income of Sven Eisenhut.

#### Answers

**A1** CHF 122 200. Calculation: 100 + 10 − 5 + 17.2 = 122.2 (in thousands). Equity = Opening equity + Increase in equity − Private withdrawals + Profit.
💡 Closing equity = Opening equity + changes in equity during year − net private withdrawals + profit of the period.

**A2** CHF 72 200. Calculation: 17.2 + 50 + 5 = 72.2 (in thousands). Owner's income = Profit + Owner's salary + Own interest (financial expenses for own capital).
💡 Owner's income includes profit plus amounts the owner took as salary and interest on own capital.

**A3** −1 000 (decrease by CHF 1 000).
💡 If interest rate goes from 5% to 6%, both bank loan interest and own interest increase by 1 percentage point. Higher bank loan interest reduces profit (expense +), but higher own interest increases owner's income. Net effect: the additional bank loan interest expense of CHF 1 000 reduces profit and thus owner's income by CHF 1 000. (Own interest cancels out since it both reduces profit and increases the owner's income add-back.)

**A4**
(a) Equity at beginning = CHF 164 000. Calculation: 210 + 32 − 20 − 110 − 8 + 60 = 164 (in thousands).
(b) Income = CHF 86 000. Calculation: 110 + 8 − 32 = 86 (salary + interest − loss).
💡 Opening equity = Closing equity + loss − equity increase − salary − interest + withdrawals. Owner's income = salary + interest − loss.

### 2.9 T-account calculations

#### Questions

**Q1** [Jan 2026] — Henry's Treasure (Sole Proprietorship, Trading)
> The following accounts are before year-end closing:
> Inventory, Trading Goods: OB 18 000 (debit)
> Expenses for Trading Goods: Debit side 390 000, 15 500; Credit side 26 500
> Sales from Trading Goods: Debit side 18 600; Credit side 720 000
> The closing balance of Trading Goods stock is CHF 21 000.
> Calculate: (a) Purchasing value of goods purchased in CHF, (b) Purchasing value of goods sold in CHF, (c) Gross Profit margin in %, (d) Gross Profit mark-up in %

**Q2** [Jan 2025] — (Trading Company)
> Before year-end closing: Inventory Trading Goods (no OB shown). Expenses for Trading Goods: Debit 950 000, 25 000; Credit 19 000. Sales from Trading Goods: Debit 120 000; Credit 1 750 000. OB inventory = CHF 22 000, CB inventory = CHF 23 000. Calculate: (a) Gross profit, (b) Purchase value of goods purchased, (c) Purchase value of goods sold, (d) Gross profit margin.

#### Answers

**A1**
(a) Purchasing value of goods purchased = CHF 379 000. Calculation: 390 + 15.5 − 26.5 = 379 (in thousands).
(b) Purchasing value of goods sold = CHF 376 000. Calculation: 390 + 15.5 − 26.5 − 3 = 376 (CB 21 − OB 18 = +3 inventory increase).
(c) Gross Profit margin = 46.39%. Calculation: ((720 − 18.6) − 376) / (720 − 18.6) = 325.4 / 701.4 = 46.39%.
(d) Gross Profit mark-up = 86.54%. Calculation: ((720 − 18.6) − 376) / 376 = 325.4 / 376 = 86.54%.
💡 Margin = Gross Profit / Net Sales. Mark-up = Gross Profit / Cost of Goods Sold. Net Sales = 720 − 18.6 (returns). COGS = Purchases net ± inventory change.

**A2**
(a) Gross profit = CHF 675 000 (1 630 000 − 955 000; where net sales = 1 750 000 − 120 000 = 1 630 000)
(b) Purchase value purchased = CHF 956 000 (955 000 + 1 000 inventory increase)
(c) Purchase value sold = CHF 955 000
(d) Gross profit margin = 41.41% (675 000 / 1 630 000)
💡 Net expenses = 950 000 + 25 000 − 19 000 = 956 000 (goods purchased). Inventory change = 23 000 − 22 000 = +1 000. COGS = 956 000 − 1 000 = 955 000.

### 4.4 Straight-line vs. declining balance

#### Questions

**Q1** [Jan 2026] — Coros Ltd.
> At the beginning of 2026, Coros Ltd. buys a new machine for CHF 400 000. Annual EBITDA is CHF 200 000. Other depreciation is CHF 50 000 annually. The new machine is depreciated starting from 2026. What is the EBIT as per 2028 if the machine is depreciated with:
> (a) straight line depreciation for 8 years?
> (b) declining balance depreciation with 25% of book value?

#### Answers

**A1**
(a) EBIT = CHF 100 000.
Straight line: 400 000 / 8 = 50 000 per year. Total depreciation 2028 = 50 000 + 50 000 = 100 000. EBIT = 200 000 − 100 000 = 100 000.
(b) EBIT = CHF 93 750.
Declining balance: 2026: 25% × 400 000 = 100 000; 2027: 25% × 300 000 = 75 000; 2028: 25% × 225 000 = 56 250. Total depreciation 2028 = 50 000 + 56 250 = 106 250. EBIT = 200 000 − 106 250 = 93 750.
💡 Straight-line gives constant annual depreciation; declining balance depreciates more in early years. EBIT = EBITDA − total depreciation (other + machine).

💡 Valuation reserves = Internal value − External value. When external depreciation > internal, reserves increase. When external < internal, reserves decrease. When equal, no change.
